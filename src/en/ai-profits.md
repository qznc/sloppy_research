title: Are OpenAI or Anthropic Profitable?
date: 2026-10-09
tldr: Anthropic achieved its first profitable quarter in Q2 2026, while OpenAI remains deeply unprofitable with losses projected through at least 2028.

The AI gold rush of 2024-2026 created multi-hundred-billion-dollar valuations for frontier model companies, with rapid revenue curves suggesting exponential growth. Yet the fundamental question remains surprisingly difficult to answer with certainty: are OpenAI and Anthropic actually making money?

The simple answer: Anthropic crossed into profitability first, while OpenAI continues burning cash at unprecedented rates.

## The Headline Numbers

Both companies achieved historic revenue growth but with dramatically different profitability outcomes.

OpenAI's revenue scaled from $1B in 2023 to $3.7B in 2024 and reached approximately $13.07B in full-year 2025, exiting the year at a $21.4B run rate (valueaddvc.com, *OpenAI Revenue 2026: ARR Reportedly Nears $70B*, 2026). By early 2026, OpenAI topped $25B in annualized revenue, about 17% higher than the $21.4B run rate at end of 2025 (The Information, *OpenAI Tops $25 Billion in Annualized Revenue as Anthropic Narrows Gap*, 2026). In Q1 2026, OpenAI generated about $5.7B in revenue, nearly $1B more than Anthropic in the same period (The Information, *OpenAI Generated Nearly $6 Billion in Revenue in First Quarter, Boosted by Codex*, 2026). By September 2026, multiple sources reported expectations of $70B ARR by year-end (Quartz, *OpenAI expects $70B annualized revenue*, 2026; Financial Post, *OpenAI expects US$70 billion in annualized revenue*, 2026).

Anthropic grew even faster in percentage terms: from $1B in annualized revenue in December 2024 to $4B by July 2025, $9B by December 2025, and surpassing $30B run-rate by October 2026 (Anthropic, *Anthropic and Amazon expand collaboration*, 2026).

## OpenAI: Record Revenue, Record Losses

### Revenue Growth

OpenAI's revenue trajectory tells the story of the fastest scaling company in tech history. Official guidance from CFO Sarah Friar in January 2026 confirmed: $2B ARR in 2023, $6B in 2024, and $20B+ in 2025 (UseLuminix, *OpenAI Revenue 2026: ~$70B ARR*, 2026). On New Year's Day 2026, OpenAI projected its monthly revenue would rise more than 50% over the first five months, roughly in line with previous investor projections (The Information, *Anthropic Is Likely Generating at Least 35% More Revenue Than OpenAI*, 2026).

By early 2026, OpenAI topped $25B in annualized revenue (The Information, *OpenAI Tops $25 Billion in Annualized Revenue as Anthropic Narrows Gap*, 2026). By February 2026, the company hit a $25B annualized run rate, which then accelerated to ~$40B by mid-August, and approached $70B by September 2026 (ValueAddVC, *OpenAI Revenue 2026: ARR Reportedly Nears $70B*, 2026). Multiple independent sources confirmed October 2026 expectations of $70B ARR by year-end (Quartz, *OpenAI expects $70B annualized revenue*, 2026; Financial Post, *OpenAI expects US$70 billion in annualized revenue*, 2026; Yahoo Finance, *OpenAI's annualized revenue nearing $70 billion*, 2026).

### The Cost Problem

Despite the revenue explosion, OpenAI's costs grew even faster. Leaked financials show a $20.9B operating loss in 2025, with a $38.5B loss figure also reported in some analyses (ValueAddVC, *OpenAI Revenue 2026*, 2026; Hacker News discussions reflecting leaked documents).

Q1 2026 showed this trend persisting: a $6.95B non-GAAP operating loss on $5.7B revenue, representing a -122% operating margin (FutureSearch.ai, *OpenAI Revenue, Losses, and IPO Valuation*, 2026). Analysts project 2026 GAAP losses near $62B, more than four times the widely cited $14B non-GAAP figure that excludes stock-based compensation (FutureSearch.ai, 2026).

OpenAI also forecast an additional $111B cash burn through 2030 according to exclusive reporting (The Information, *Exclusive: OpenAI Boost Revenue Forecasts, Predicts $111 Billion More Cash Burn Through 2030*, 2026).

Unit economics also deteriorated. Gross margins declined from 40% in 2024 to 33% in 2025, moving away from sustainable software economics (Klover.ai, *Is AI Profitable: OpenAI's Path Toward Profit*, 2026). This compares unfavorably to Anthropic's own margins, which SemiAnalysis reports increased from 38% to over 70% during the same period due to improved inference efficiency (SemiAnalysis, *AI Value Capture - The Shift To Model Labs*, 2026). Inference costs alone reached $8.4B in 2025 and are projected to rise to $14.1B in 2026 (Sacra, *OpenAI revenue, valuation & funding*, 2026). SemiAnalysis further notes that OpenAI's need to support over 900M free users creates additional burden, with estimated monthly service costs of approximately $0.70 per person (SemiAnalysis, 2026).

### Profitability Timeline

OpenAI has told investors it expects to reach profitability in 2030 after spending more than $600B on computing infrastructure (Yahoo Finance, *Anthropic on Track for First Profitable Quarter*, 2026). The company forecasts cash-flow-positive operations by 2029, with approximately $200B in revenue by 2030.

## Anthropic: From Rapid Growth to Profitability

### Revenue Explosion

Anthropic demonstrated even more rapid growth in percentage terms. In December 2025, Anthropic projected generating as much as $18B in 2026, about 20% more than its summer forecast, and $55B in 2027 (The Information, *Anthropic Increases 2026 Revenue Forecast by 20% to $18 Billion*, 2026). By October 2026, Anthropic's run-rate revenue had surpassed $30B, up from approximately $9B at the end of 2025 (Anthropic, *Anthropic and Amazon expand collaboration*, 2026). Some analysts noted Anthropic was likely generating at least 35% more revenue than OpenAI at certain points in 2026 (The Information, *Anthropic Is Likely Generating at Least 35% More Revenue Than OpenAI*, 2026).

Pacing was extraordinary: Q2 2026 projections showed revenue more than doubling from Q1: $10.9B in Q2 2026 compared to $4.8B in Q1 2026 (Yahoo Finance, *Anthropic on Track for First Profitable Quarter*, 2026). SemiAnalysis reports Anthropic's ARR surged from $9B at the end of 2025 to over $44B currently, with gross margins on inference infrastructure increasing from 38% to over 70% (SemiAnalysis, *AI Value Capture - The Shift To Model Labs*, 2026).

### The Path to Profit

Anthropic achieved a milestone in Q2 2026: an operating profit of $559M on $10.9B revenue (Yahoo Finance, 2026). SemiAnalysis analysis project Anthropic to achieve GAAP EBIT of $1B in Q3 2026, representing approximately 6% profit margin (HTX Insights summarizing SemiAnalysis, *SemiAnalysis: Anthropic's Q3 Profits Set to Exceed $1 Billion*, 2026). This represented the company's first profitable quarter, with expectations of continued profitability according to SemiAnalysis forecasts (SemiAnalysis, *Anthropic 3Q26 Profit Over $1B: The Anthropic IPO Financials Sneak Peak*, 2026).

This result comes as Anthropic was on the cusp of closing a $30B funding round at a $900B valuation. However, despite the improved revenue outlook, Anthropic delayed when it would go cash flow positive (The Information, *Anthropic Hikes 2026 Revenue Forecast 20% but Delays When It Will Go Cash Flow Positive*, 2026). By comparison, OpenAI has told investors it expects to reach profitability in 2030.

### Compute Infrastructure Commitments

Anthropic has committed to spending more than $100B on Amazon Web Services technologies over the next 10 years, securing up to 5 gigawatts of capacity for training and deploying Claude models (Anthropic, 2026; AboutAmazon, *Amazon invests additional $5 billion in Anthropic*, 2026).

Amazon's corresponding investment includes $5B in April 2026, with up to an additional $20B in the future, building on the $8B previously invested (AboutAmazon, 2026). The capacity spans Trainium2 through Trainium4 chips, with significant Trainium3 capacity expected by end of 2026.

These infrastructure commitments reflect the massive scale required to stay competitive in frontier AI while also illustrating the structural cost pressures that make profitability challenging.

## Comparison: Why the Profitability Gap?

### Similar Growth, Different Outcomes

Both companies achieved historic revenue growth rates. OpenAI grew from $1B in 2023 to potentially $70B ARR in 2026. Anthropic grew from $1B in late 2024 to $30B+ in 2026. Yet their profitability diverged notably: Anthropic is the clear clubhouse leader in capturing the B2B market, doing so profitably against an unfocused and money-burning competitor (SemiAnalysis, *Anthropic 3Q26 Profit Over $1B: The Anthropic IPO Financials Sneak Peak*, 2026).

### Cost Control and Unit Economics

The critical difference lies in cost efficiency. OpenAI's gross margins declined from 40% to 33% between 2024-2025, while facing a -122% operating margin in Q1 2026 (FutureSearch.ai, 2026; Klover.ai, 2026). This deterioration contrasts sharply with Anthropic, whose gross margins on inference infrastructure increased from 38% to over 70% between 2025-2026 (SemiAnalysis, *AI Value Capture - The Shift To Model Labs*, 2026). The margin divergence is partly explained by OpenAI's need to support over 900 million free users, creating an estimated $0.70 per person monthly service cost burden (SemiAnalysis, 2026).

SemiAnalysis also notes that paying 20-30% of revenue share to hyperscale cloud platforms remains economically justifiable from the perspective of enterprise customer reach and compliance efficiency, with both companies operating under similar cloud dependency structures (SemiAnalysis, 2026).

### Infrastructure Dependencies

Both companies remain heavily dependent on their respective cloud partners. OpenAI relies primarily on Microsoft Azure, while Anthropic uses AWS as its primary training and cloud provider (AboutAmazon, 2026). Analysts note that cloud providers typically capture 16-32% of AI startup revenue before companies can achieve profitability (Intellectia.ai, *Morgan Stanley Reiterates Amazon as Top AI Winner*, 2026).

This creates a structural constraint: as AI companies scale, a substantial portion of each additional revenue dollar flows directly to cloud infrastructure costs, delaying the path to profitability.

## Steelman: The Counter-Arguments

### "They must be profitable given their valuations"

The argument: If OpenAI is valued at $500-852B and Anthropic at $380-900B, they must be generating profits.

The steelman: At typical software company multiples of 10-20x revenue, these valuations would require $5-17T in annual revenue to justify. OpenAI's projected $70B ARR and Anthropic's $30B+ run-rate fall dramatically short. This suggests investors are betting either on unprecedented future growth or that current valuations reflect strategic value beyond traditional profitability metrics.

The counter-evidence: Despite Anthropic's profitability milestone, questions remain about valuation sustainability at current multiples. The breadth of the gap between revenue and valuation for both companies indicates that traditional metrics are not the primary drivers of current pricing.

### "Big Tech backing means they don't need profitability"

The argument: Microsoft and Amazon investments mean profitability is irrelevant for these companies.

The steelman: For OpenAI, Microsoft's agreement reportedly entitles it to 75% of all profits until its investment is recovered. For Anthropic, Amazon captures immediate value through infrastructure spending. This creates an asymmetric relationship where cloud providers benefit regardless of AI company profitability.

The counter-evidence: However, commercial viability still matters for sustaining growth and justifying ongoing investment. The local minima of positive contribution margins is what matters in the long run. Anthropic's achievement demonstrates that profitability is both possible and valuable, even within the Big Tech ecosystem.

### "Revenue growth implies profitability is imminent"

The argument: Growth rates of 200%+ suggest profitability must follow quickly.

The steelman: Revenue growth can mask deteriorating unit economics. OpenAI's Q1 2026 operating margin of -122% demonstrates that each additional dollar of revenue may cost more than a dollar to generate, as inference costs scale with usage and model complexity.

The counter-evidence: Anthropic's Q2 2026 profitability shows that breaking through to positive margins is achievable, but it requires both growth and cost discipline. The key challenge is whether cost control can keep pace with scaling demand.

### "Secrecy means they're hiding profits"

The argument: The lack of transparency suggests hidden profitability.

The steelman: AI companies maintain secrecy primarily for competitive reasons—protecting model details, training methods, and infrastructure advantages. However, the available financial disclosures from official guidance, investor updates, and third-party analysis consistently point away from current profitability for OpenAI and toward cautious optimism for Anthropic.

The counter-evidence: Both companies have provided sufficient official and third-party verified data to establish their respective profitability statuses. The question is not whether the information exists, but how to interpret it properly.

### "They're burning cash on R&D, not operations"

The argument: The heavy spending reflects investment in future capabilities, not operational losses.

The distinction: OpenAI's $600B+ planned infrastructure spending through the decade represents capital investment in compute and data centers, not operating expenses. However, even accounting for this R&D framing, the company projects GAAP losses of ~$62B in 2026 and expects to reach profitability only by 2030.

The counter-evidence: Anthropic's Q2 2026 profitability, achieved while maintaining aggressive growth and infrastructure commitments, demonstrates that the distinction matters less than the overall financial trajectory.

## The Structural Challenge

The core issue facing AI companies is that each additional dollar of revenue requires significant incremental compute and infrastructure costs. Traditional software economics, where margins expand with scale, do not apply cleanly in the current AI paradigm.

For OpenAI, this resulted in operating losses exceeding revenue in 2025 and a negative 122% operating margin in Q1 2026. For Anthropic, while Q2 2026 showed profitability, the sustainability depends on maintaining cost discipline while continuing to scale.

Cloud dependencies add another layer: major cloud providers capture substantial value before AI companies see the benefits. Whether this is through Microsoft's profit-sharing with OpenAI or Amazon's infrastructure revenue from Anthropic, the cloud layer extracts meaningful margin.

## What We Know with High Confidence

**OpenAI:** Not profitable as of October 2026. Revenue: $13.07B (2025) growing to ~$50-70B ARR (2026). Losses: $20.9B operating loss in 2025, -122% operating margin in Q1 2026. Gross margins: declining from 40% to 33%. Profitability target: 2030.

**Anthropic:** First profitable quarter in Q2 2026 ($559M operating profit on $10.9B revenue). Revenue: $9B ARR (end 2025) to $30B+ run-rate (Oct 2026). AWS commitment: $100B+ over 10 years.

## What Remains Uncertain

The pace at which OpenAI can improve its unit economics remains unclear. Anthropic's ability to sustain profitability beyond Q2 2026 also requires verification. Both companies operate with unprecedented scale and structural challenges that make traditional financial forecasting difficult.

## Conclusion

The surprising difficulty in answering this question definitively stems from the companies' rapid evolution, coupled with the structural economics of AI development. What is clear is that for OpenAI, the path to profitability remains years away despite enormous revenue growth. Anthropic has crossed the profitability threshold first, proving it is possible even amidst the pressures of scaling frontier AI.

The broader implication is that AI company profitability is not simply a matter of reaching sufficient scale. It requires solving the unit economics challenge of delivering AI services efficiently, even as the cost and complexity of those services continue to rise with advancements in model capability and user demand.
